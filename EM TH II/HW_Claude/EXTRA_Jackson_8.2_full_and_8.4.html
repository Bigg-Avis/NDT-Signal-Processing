Jackson 8.2 — Coaxial transmission line
A transmission line of two concentric metal cylinders (conductivity σ, skin depth δ), inner radius a, outer radius b, filled with a lossless dielectric (μ, ε), carrying a TEM mode. H0 is the peak azimuthal magnetic field at the surface of the inner conductor. Find (a) the time-averaged power flow, (b) the attenuation, (c) the characteristic impedance, (d) the series resistance and inductance per unit length.
ANSWER
(a)  P = √μ/ε πa2|H0|2 ln(b/a)
(b)  P(z) = P0e−2γz,   γ = 12σδ √ε/μ (1/a + 1/b)ln(b/a)
(c)  Z0 = 12π √μ/ε ln(b/a)
(d)  R = 12πσδ (1a + 1b),   L = μ2πln(b/a) + μcδ4π (1a + 1b)
Flow: get the TEM fields from Lec 10 → integrate the Poynting vector for P → integrate the wall loss for γ and R → integrate E and H for Z0 → integrate the magnetic energy for L. Every part is one integral over the same two fields.
Step 0  —  The TEM fields in a coax
Lec 10 gives the TEM mode: with Bz = 0 and Ez = 0,
Et = −∇tφ,     ∇t2φ = 0,     k = ω√με Lec 10, s13–15
So it is a 2D electrostatics problem in the cross-section. For two concentric circles the solution depends on ρ only:
φ(ρ) = A ln ρ + B   ⇒   Eρ = − dφdρ = −Aρ everything ∝ 1/ρ
For a TEM wave H is azimuthal and tied to E by the wave impedance Z = √μ/ε. Writing H0 for its value at ρ = a:
Hφ = H0 aρ,     Eρ = √μ/ε H0 aρ
These two lines are the whole of Step 0 — every part of the problem is an integral of them. The 1/ρ is forced by ∇t2φ = 0 in cylindrical symmetry; it is the same 1/ρ as the field of a line charge.
2
PART (a)  — time-averaged power flow along the line
Step 1  —  The Poynting vector
Power flows along z. The time-averaged Poynting vector for complex amplitudes is
⟨S⟩ = 12 Re( E × H* ) Jackson §6.9
With E radial and H azimuthal, ρ̂ × φ̂ = ẑ, so the flow is purely along the line:
⟨Sz⟩ = 12 EρHφ* = 12 √μ/ε |H0|2 a2ρ2
Step 2  —  Integrate over the cross-section
The dielectric fills the annulus a ≤ ρ ≤ b. A ring of radius ρ has area 2πρ dρ:
P = ∫ab 12 √μ/ε |H0|2 a2ρ2  2πρ dρ
The ρ2 downstairs cancels one ρ upstairs, leaving dρ/ρ:
= π√μ/ε |H0|2a2 ∫ab dρρ = π√μ/ε |H0|2a2  ln(ba)
That is where the ln(b/a) comes from — it is just ∫dρ/ρ. It appears again in (c) and (d) for the same reason.
ANSWER — PART (a)
P = √μ/ε πa2 |H0|2 ln (ba)

PART (b)  — attenuation along the line
Step 1  —  Why the power decays at all
The dielectric is lossless, so the only loss is ohmic heating in the metal walls. The wall is not a perfect conductor, so the surface field drives a current in a skin layer of depth δ.
dPlossdA = 12σδ |H∥|2 power dissipated per unit wall area Jackson §8.1
Step 2  —  Add up both walls
The tangential field at each wall is Hφ evaluated there, and each wall contributes its own circumference per unit length:
wall |H∥| circumference loss per unit length
inner, ρ = aH0 2πa πa H02/σδ
outer, ρ = bH0a/b 2πb πa2H02/σδb
−dPdz = πa2H02σδ (1a + 1b) a2 factored out of both rows
Step 3  —  Convert a loss rate into an exponent
If P(z) = P0e−2γz, then differentiating gives −dP/dz = 2γP. So
γ = −dP/dz2P
Divide the Step 2 result by twice the part (a) answer. The πa2H02 cancels completely:
γ = πa2H02(1/a + 1/b)/σδ2√μ/ε πa2H02 ln(b/a)
ANSWER — PART (b)
γ = 12σδ √ε/μ 1/a + 1/bln(b/a)
1/√μ/ε was written as √ε/μ.

PART (c)  — characteristic impedance Z0 = V/I
The problem defines Z0 as the voltage between the cylinders divided by the axial current in one of them. So compute each separately from the Step 0 fields.
Step 1  —  The voltage
Integrate the radial field across the gap:
V = ∫ab Eρ dρ = √μ/ε H0a ∫ab dρρ = √μ/ε H0 a ln(b/a)
Step 2  —  The current
Ampère's law round the inner conductor — Hφ is constant on that circle:
I = ∮ H·dl = H0 · 2πa
Step 3  —  Divide
Z0 = VI = √μ/ε H0 a ln(b/a)2πa H0 H0 and a both cancel
ANSWER — PART (c)
Z0 = 12π √μ/ε ln (ba)
Depends only on the geometry ratio b/a and the dielectric — not on a and b separately, and not on frequency. That is why coax is sold as “50 Ω” regardless of cable thickness.

PART (d)  — series resistance and inductance per unit length
Step 1  —  R from the wall loss
A resistance R per unit length carrying peak current I dissipates 12R|I|2 per unit length. Set that equal to the wall loss already computed in part (b):
12R |I|2 = πa2H02σδ (1a + 1b),     |I|2 = (2πa H0)2 = 4π2a2H02
Solve for R — a2H02 cancels:
R = 2πa2H02(1/a+1/b)/σδ4π2a2H02 = 12πσδ (1a + 1b)
This is just “resistance = resistivity × length / area” for a conductor whose effective thickness is the skin depth δ: each wall carries current in a strip of width 2πa (or 2πb) and depth δ, and the two are in series, so their resistances add.

Part (d), continued
Step 2  —  L from the stored magnetic energy
An inductance L per unit length stores 14L|I|2 on time average. The energy sits in two places:
(i) In the dielectric, between the conductors:
Wext = μ4 ∫ab |Hφ|2 2πρ dρ = πμa2H022 ln(b/a) same ∫dρ/ρ as part (a)
(ii) Inside the metal, in the skin layer. The field decays as Hse−x/δ going into the wall, so per unit wall area:
μc4 ∫0∞ Hs2e−2x/δ dx = μcδHs28 ∫e−2x/δdx = δ/2
Add both walls, as in part (b):
Wint = πμcδa2H024 (1a + 1b)
Set 14L|I|2 = Wext + Wint and divide by π2a2H02:
ANSWER — PART (d)
R = 12πσδ (1a + 1b)
L = μ2πln (ba) + μcδ4π (1a + 1b)
First term = flux between the conductors. Second = flux that leaks a depth δ into them — exactly the correction the question mentions.

Jackson 8.4 — Hollow circular waveguide
TE and TM waves propagate along a hollow circular cylinder of inner radius R and conductivity σ. (a) Find the cutoff frequencies of the TE and TM modes; determine the lowest (dominant) one and the ratios of the next four higher modes to it, assuming σ → ∞. (b) Calculate the attenuation constants for the lowest two distinct modes and plot them against frequency.
ANSWER
(a)  ωc = xR√με,   x = xmn (TM: Jm zero)  or  x′mn (TE: J′m zero)
dominant = TE11, x′11 = 1.841  →  ωc = 1.841/(R√με)
next four: TM01 1.306, TE21 1.659, TE01 and TM11 2.081 (degenerate)
(b)  αTM = RsRη 1√1 − (ωc/ω)2
αTE = RsRη 1√1 − (ωc/ω)2 [ (ωcω)2 + m2x′2 − m2 ]
Rs = 1/σδ = √μcω/2σ (surface resistance), η = √μ/ε.
Flow: the lecture already gives the 2D equation and the wall condition for each mode. In a circle those become Bessel equations → the wall condition picks out Bessel zeros → each zero is one mode with its own cutoff.
8
PART (a)  — cutoff frequencies
Step 1  —  The two mode equations, from lecture
Lec 10 has already reduced each mode to a 2D problem in the cross-section:
TM (Bz = 0):   ∇t2Ez + γ2Ez = 0,    Ez = 0 on the wall Lec 10, s17
TE (Ez = 0):   ∇t2Bz + γ2Bz = 0,    ∂Bz/∂n = 0 on the wall Lec 10, s18
γ2 = μεω2 − k2 the same γ in both Lec 10, s17
The only difference between TE and TM is the wall condition: the function itself vanishes for TM, its normal derivative vanishes for TE. Everything else below is identical.
Step 2  —  A circle forces cylindrical coordinates
Write ψ for whichever of Ez or Bz applies. In polar coordinates
∇t2 = 1ρ ∂∂ρ (ρ ∂∂ρ) + 1ρ2 ∂2∂φ2
Separate, ψ = f(ρ)e imφ. m must be an integer, otherwise ψ would not come back to itself after going once round the circle:
f″ + 1ρf′ + (γ2 − m2ρ2)f = 0 Bessel’s equation
Its two solutions are Jm(γρ) and Ym(γρ). The guide is hollow with no inner conductor, so ρ = 0 is inside the region — and Ym blows up there. Keep only
ψ = Jm(γρ) e imφ
This is the one place Bessel functions are unavoidable — they are simply “sine and cosine for a circle”. In a rectangular guide the same separation gives ordinary sines, which is the case done in Lec 9, s17.
Step 3  —  The wall condition picks out the zeros
TM:  ψ(R) = 0 ⇒ Jm(γR) = 0 ⇒ γR = xmn nth zero of Jm
TE:  ∂ψ/∂ρ|R = 0 ⇒ J′m(γR) = 0 ⇒ γR = x′mn nth zero of J′m
Each zero is one mode. That is what m and n label.

Part (a), continued
Step 4  —  From γ to the cutoff frequency
The wave propagates only if k is real, i.e. k2 > 0:
k2 = μεω2 − γ2 > 0   ⇒   ω > γ√με
Below that, k is imaginary and the field dies away instead of travelling. The changeover is the cutoff:
ωc = γ√με = xR√με x = xmn for TM, x′mn for TE
Smaller x → lower cutoff. So ranking the modes just means ranking the Bessel zeros.

TE213.05421.659

ANSWER — PART (a)
dominant: TE11,   ωc = 1.841R√με
next four:  1.306 (TM01),  1.659 (TE21),  2.081 (TE01 and TM11)
TE01 and TM11 are degenerate — a coincidence of Bessel functions, J′0 = −J1, so the zeros of J′0 and J1 are the same numbers. For R = 1 cm in vacuum, the dominant cutoff is 8.785 GHz.

PART (b)  — attenuation of the lowest two modes
The two lowest distinct modes are TE11 (x′ = 1.841) and TM01 (x = 2.405), from the table above.
Step 1  —  Same method as 8.2(b)
Wall loss over power flow — identical logic, only the fields differ:
α = −dP/dz2P,     −dPdz = Rs2 ∮|H∥|2 dl,     Rs = 1σδ = √μcω/2σ Jackson §8.1
Carrying out both integrals with the Bessel fields gives:
αTM = RsRη 1√1 − (ωc/ω)2
αTE = RsRη 1√1 − (ωc/ω)2 [ (ωcω)2 + m2x′2 − m2 ]
For our two modes: TM01 has m = 0, so no bracket. TE11 has m = 1 and x′ = 1.841, so the extra term is 1/(1.8412−1) = 0.419.
Step 2  —  Read the shape
1 Both diverge at cutoff — 1/√1−(ωc/ω)2 → ∞ as ω → ωc.
2 Far above cutoff Rs ∝ √ω, so both rise again.
3 So each has a minimum in between — the best operating frequency.
11
Part (b), continued
ANSWER — PART (b)
TE11: minimum α = 0.0063 Np/m at ω/ωc = 3.16  (27.7 GHz)
TM01: minimum α = 0.0119 Np/m at ω/ωc = 1.73  (19.8 GHz)
TE11 is the lower-loss mode over the whole band — which, with its lower cutoff, is why circular guides are run in TE11.
